Opening factors

Grey figures are guesses taken from community guides for a launch-window build of FM26. Green figures are measured from your own closed deals and override the guess once there are three of them.

Why it strips before it haggles

  • reportedFM26 agents barely move on base wage but will drop or remove peripheral demands like appearance fees. So the wage ladder is short and the stripping does the work.
  • reportedBonuses don't count against your wage budget, which is why they get waved through and then quietly outgrow the wage.
  • reportedA longer deal and a higher squad status both pull the wage down without costing you cash.
  • reportedRaising the agent fee instead of the wage converts a recurring cost into a one-off.
  • reportedWalking away and returning about a week later often brings a softer demand.

Where it could be wrong

  • assumedRemoval order: unused sub fee, then appearance fee, then wage rise, then goal and assist bonuses. Ranked by how much each one costs when the signing works out, not by tested agent resistance.
  • assumedConcession order when the ladder dies: agent fee, signing-on fee, loyalty bonus.
  • assumed52-week years and no mid-contract status changes. Bonus costs are a range, not a prediction: the floor is a bad season, the ceiling is your full fixture list. The ceiling is the honest number, since nothing caps a bonus.
  • reportedThere's a negotiation bug where editing anything snaps a softened demand back to the original figure. If the agent volunteers a low wage, take it rather than counter.
  • Sell-on clauses are deliberately not used as a sweetener here. They tax the exit, and the exit is the point.
